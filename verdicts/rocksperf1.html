Setup: MyRocks (RocksDB block cache 48 GB), 400 warehouses, HammerDB TPC-C (80 VUs) plus bash malloc-stress clients; steady-only runs (10 m rampup + 30 m measured); 3 repetitions per configuration. Note: performance_schema does not instrument RocksDB’s internal allocations (tracked bytes ≈ 0.7 GB), so the “overhead” metric is not meaningful here — compare RSS directly. The thp and nothp halves ran concurrently on separate hosts, so THP percentages carry a host variable.

  • Unlike InnoDB, allocator choice moves MyRocks throughput dramatically — the spread is 27 % (vs ≤1 % for InnoDB on the same hardware). RocksDB allocates its block cache, memtables and compaction buffers in many small-to-medium chunks through malloc, so the allocator sits on the hot path in a way InnoDB’s one-big-buffer-pool design avoids.
  • jemalloc 3.6 + THP is the standout: 8,641 TPS, +27 % over the slowest and +14 % over the next-best THP configuration. It is the only allocator whose memory gets almost fully huge-page-backed (53.7 GB AnonHugePages vs 6.6–17 GB for the others) — its 2–4 MB-aligned chunks are khugepaged-friendly, while the others leave most of the block cache on 4 KB pages.
  • Without THP, jemalloc 3.6 also leads (7,646 TPS), ahead of glibc (7,503), jemalloc 5.3 (7,020) and tcmalloc (6,782) — tcmalloc, the InnoDB winner, is the slowest allocator under MyRocks (−11 % vs jemalloc 3.6).
  • RSS varies hugely by allocator: tcmalloc 49.0 GB (leanest), jemalloc 3.6 50.7–52.5 GB, glibc 56.6 GB, jemalloc 5.3 59.6–60.6 GB — 23 % more physical memory than tcmalloc for the same 48 GB cache, and slow on top of it.
  • THP helps every allocator here (+0.7 % glibc, +8 % jemalloc 5.3, +11 % tcmalloc, +13 % jemalloc 3.6), roughly in proportion to how much memory actually gets huge-page-backed.

Bottom line: for MyRocks the allocator decision is first-order, and it inverts the InnoDB ranking: jemalloc 3.6 with THP always is the clear winner (fastest by a wide margin, mid-pack RSS), validating Percona’s jemalloc guidance for MyRocks; tcmalloc trades 11–13 % throughput for the smallest footprint; jemalloc 5.3 is the worst of both worlds here (slowest-but-one and the largest RSS); glibc is mediocre throughout and barely benefits from THP. Worth a follow-up: jemalloc 5.3 with opt.thp/metadata_thp or 2 MB-aligned extent tuning to see if it can recover the 3.6 behavior.
