Setup: 20-hour HammerDB TPC-C runs at ~2400 MHz with a 150 GB buffer pool, /proc/pid/maps sampled every few seconds (~21k snapshots per run).

  • glibc is the address-space outlier: a constant 5.95 GB of PROT_NONE reserved arena space (all other allocators ~0), the most VMAs (~2,040), nearly double the mapping churn of tcmalloc, and the lowest committed-to-mapped efficiency (96.5 % vs 99.9 %).
  • jemalloc 5.3 is the leanest on mappings (~1,333 VMAs, lowest churn) but keeps the largest committed virtual footprint (~170 GB, of which ~7 GB is not resident — decay purging returns pages while keeping ranges mapped; cosmetic for VSZ-based monitoring).
  • tcmalloc has the tightest virtual/physical correspondence (committed ≈ resident, no reserves, lowest churn).
  • All configurations are flat over 20 hours — committed growth ≤0.06 GB; no maps-level leak or creep at this load.
  • The THP axis of this dataset is void: AnonHugePages is 0 kB in every snapshot of every run — THP was in madvise mode and never engaged, so the thp/nothp pairs are effectively duplicate runs (usefully, they calibrate the noise floor: identical configurations differ by 0.2–1.3 % NOPM). Later suites fixed this by setting THP via sysfs and verifying AnonHugePages in-run.

Bottom line: under a huge stable buffer pool no allocator misbehaves on RSS, but glibc is the worst address-space citizen; the differences here are virtual-memory hygiene, not physical footprint (see the smaps report for residency).
