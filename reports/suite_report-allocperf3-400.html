Data source: run_allocator_perf_suite.sh results in suite-allocperf3-400. Phases per run: steady load → idle → regrow. Time-series lines are the mean across repetitions (solid = THP enabled, dashed = THP disabled; color = allocator). Bars are the median across repetitions.

Verdict

Setup: 400 warehouses (~40 GB, fully cached in the 48 GB buffer pool), HammerDB TPC-C (80 VUs) plus sysbench malloc-stress scripts (complex queries with randomized sort/join/temp buffers, prepared-statement churn, connect/disconnect cycling, internal-allocation stress); phases steady → idle → regrow; 3–6 repetitions per configuration; THP engagement verified in-run (51–52 GB AnonHugePages in thp runs).

  • tcmalloc is the fastest in both THP modes (11,102 TPS steady with THP; rep range 11,101..11,103). Its lead over the other allocators is ~0.4 % — small, but outside the ±0.1–0.3 % rep noise.
  • THP always is worth +1.6–1.8 % for every allocator. (Caveat: the thp and nothp halves ran concurrently on separate hosts, so the THP percentages carry a host variable; allocator rankings within each mode do not.)
  • The sysbench complex-query axis does not separate allocators (≤1.3 %, within noise); THP helps it by +2–4 %.
  • Memory reproduces the series-wide signature: glibc carries ~700 MB more untracked overhead than the field and a ~450 MB/cycle plateau ratchet; jemalloc 3.6 is the leanest (1.68 GB overhead, near-zero ratchet); jemalloc 5.3 is clean without THP but its overhead inflates to ~2.5 GB with it; tcmalloc is mode-invariant and near-ratchet-free (≤54 MB). With connection-churning stress, every allocator (including glibc) now releases some memory at idle.

Series verdict (four datasets: 150G-pool steady, 400wh bash-stress, 800wh I/O-mixed, 400wh sysbench-stress): the ranking is stable — tcmalloc for best throughput and the most predictable memory in every shape tested; jemalloc 3.6 for minimum RSS at a ~0.3–2 % throughput cost; jemalloc 5.3 fine without THP but pays a visible memory penalty with it; glibc malloc throughput-competitive but structurally the worst memory citizen (highest overhead, compounding plateau growth) — avoid it for long-running instances with variable load. THP always is consistently +1.5–2 % when the working set is cached.
