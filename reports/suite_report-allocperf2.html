Allocator Performance Suite Report

Data source: run_allocator_perf_suite.sh results in suite-allocperf2. Phases per run: steady load → idle → regrow. Time-series lines are the mean across repetitions (solid = THP enabled, dashed = THP disabled; color = allocator). Bars are the median across repetitions.

Verdict

Setup: 400 warehouses (~40 GB, fully cached in the 48 GB buffer pool), HammerDB TPC-C (80 VUs) plus bash malloc-stress clients (big sorts, GROUP_CONCAT, prepared-statement churn, connection cycling); phases steady → idle → regrow; 3 repetitions per configuration. Runs are CPU-bound (~11k TPS vs ~6.3k when the dataset exceeded the pool) and the rep noise floor is 0.1–0.7 %, so sub-1 % differences are resolvable. THP engaged fully (51–53 GB AnonHugePages in thp runs).

  • tcmalloc leads TPM without THP (+0.7 % over glibc, +1.0 % over the jemallocs — above noise). Under THP the field is a near-tie (tcmalloc ≈ jemalloc 5.3 ≈ glibc within 0.1 %): huge pages level the TLB-miss component that allocator layout otherwise influences.
  • THP always is worth +1.4–2.2 % for every allocator (caveat: thp and nothp halves ran concurrently on separate hosts).
  • glibc is the memory outlier: highest RSS and ~500–800 MB more untracked overhead (RSS minus performance_schema tracked bytes) than the rest.
  • The jemallocs shrink across the idle/regrow cycle without THP (ratchet −25/−27 MB) — the cleanest release-to-OS behavior; under THP both pick up idle-phase growth (+360–560 MB), consistent with khugepaged collapsing sparse regions into fully-resident huge pages. tcmalloc is unaffected either way.
  • Regrow TPM is uniformly ~2.5–2.8 % below steady in every configuration — dataset growth plus purge backlog make the second loaded phase inherently slower; it does not affect the ranking.

Bottom line: tcmalloc if throughput is the priority (edge is real but ~1 %, and disappears under THP); jemalloc for the leanest, best-behaved memory; glibc consistently the worst memory citizen despite competitive throughput.
